## Notes

- The meeting is being recorded.  Our code of conduct applies to this meeting.  https://github.com/Ecma-TC54/tg2/blob/main/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md.

- Summary
  - Task Group 2 agreed on a plan for completing the preparation of the updated PURL standard (ECMA-427) and the new VERS standards to Ecma by September including finalized naming conventions and specification strategies for PURL and VERS standards.
  - The group formally adopted 'software-id' as the standard terminology for the proposed new PURL type formerly known as 'sid'.
  - The VERS specification will mandate canonical inputs for clause 5, while relegating normalization details to recommended test categories.
  - The team approved upgrading to JSON schema 2020-12 to leverage enhanced schema capabilities.
  - Future updates will focus on clear distinctions between required and recommended parsing behaviors and documenting normalization procedures.
  - Participants discussed offering office hours for community engagement.
- **Meeting Opening and Housekeeping**: Michael Herzog opened the meeting of TC54 task group 2, confirming that the session is recorded solely for the purpose of generating minutes via Gemini. The meeting operates under the ECMA code of conduct, and the minutes from the previous meeting were reviewed and approved.
- **Agenda and URI Scheme Approval**: The primary business for the meeting is to review plans for August to complete work on the PURL and the VERS specifications. Michael Herzog announced that "pkg" was approved by IANA as a provisional URI scheme, and a similar process will be followed for "vers" once the standard is published. Michael Herzog also noted a decision to use "software-id" instead of "sid" to avoid confusion with existing SWID tags and plans to take over the pending pull request regarding this type registration next week.
- **Software ID Terminology Debate**: Van Lindberg and Michael Herzog discussed the naming preference between "sid" and "software-id". While Van Lindberg suggested that "sid" was more concise, Michael Herzog argued that "software-id" is descriptive and clearer for the user. The group agreed to proceed with "software-id," with Michael Herzog emphasizing that the goal is to make type names as descriptive as possible.
- **Preparation for TC54 Meeting**: Michael Herzog and Steve Springett discussed the upcoming TC54 meeting in two weeks, noting that the agenda will be crowded. To maximize efficiency, they agreed to distribute materials for asynchronous review in advance, similar to the CycloneDX process.
- **Specification Structure and Canonical Inputs**: Discussion moved to the VERS specification draft, specifically clause 5. Michael Herzog emphasized a strict approach for the VERS specification, requiring canonical inputs and relegating normalization information to "recommended" rather than "required" tests. Steve Springett advised prioritizing the normative text, noting that non-normative editorial changes can be submitted after the September 1st deadline.
- **VERS Type Schema and Documentation Strategy**: Michael Herzog and Steve Springett reviewed the plan for the VERS type schema, which will be published as normative in clause 6 and Annex A. They discussed creating "notes" documents for each type to provide context, such as history or considerations, rather than rewriting existing external documentation.
- **Grandfathering and Automation**: The plan includes grandfathering 15 VERS types that match PURL types and 7 special VERS types. Michael Herzog confirmed the transition to the 2020-12 version of the JSON Schema (https://json-schema.org/draft/2020-12), utilizing features such as "prefixItems". The team is automating the process so that updates to the specification repositories are automatically reflected on the website (https://packageurl.org/), allowing them to strip out front-matter comments during publication.
- **Project Timeline and Engagement**: Philippe Ombredanne confirmed that the review of the VERS specification would be completed by the middle of next week. Michael Herzog plans to engage with CSAF representatives, Thomas Schmidt and Immanuel Kunz, to ensure their feedback on canonical inputs is incorporated into the plan.
- **PURL Specification and Test Suites**: The group discussed refining the PURL specification, specifically clarifying "shall" versus "should" requirements and updating the test suite to use "required" and "recommended" categories. There is ongoing debate regarding whether normalization rules should be required or recommended; Philippe Ombredanne noted that complete canonicalization is difficult without fully implementing version type definitions. Michael Herzog proposed that if normalization is performed, there should be a message indicating that the input was normalized.
- **Ecosystem Standardization**: Michael Herzog and Philippe Ombredanne discussed the challenges of handling inconsistent naming conventions across ecosystems like NuGet. Philippe Ombredanne suggested that long-term coordination between maintainers of various package ecosystems might be necessary to address these complexities.
- **Qualifiers and ABNF Grammar**: The meeting addressed the potential standardization of qualifiers, specifically regarding lexicographical sorting and key standardization. Michael Herzog noted that the ABNF grammar work is pending, depending on the availability of Jan Kowalleck to move it forward.
- **Enumerations and JSON Schema Upgrades**: A proposal to add enumerations for namespaces and qualifiers in the PURL type definition was discussed. The consensus is to categorize these as "recommended" rather than "required" to avoid causing failures for non-standard but syntactically correct inputs. The group also reaffirmed the upgrade to JSON Schema 2020-12.
- **Versioning**: The group plans to tag the next PURL release as version 1.1 for test schema, treating it as a minor change rather than a breaking change.
- **Newsletter Service**: Michael Herzog is setting up an open-source, hosted newsletter service to highlight these changes on the website.
- **GitHub Project Management**: Michael Herzog clarified that only issues are tracked in the GitHub project, with pull requests shown in the main table. Steve Springett requested to add a comment to issue 690 (https://github.com/package-url/purl-spec/issues/690) regarding extensibility, noting that the plan is "doable".
- **Rolling vs Versioned Specification**: Steve Springett and Philippe Ombredanne agreed that the PURL specification should be versioned rather than using a "rolling spec" model, citing the need for stability.
- **Release Cycles and Governance**: Michael Herzog expressed a preference for simple Semantic Versioning (SemVer) for the standards, where significant changes like test suite updates would trigger a minor release. They acknowledged the need for further governance and stronger community consensus for proposed changes.
- **Industrial Use Cases and Modified Packages**: Philippe Ombredanne raised the common industrial issue of patching packages (e.g., backporting fixes for CVEs) without upgrading versions. There is a need to provide clear guidance on signaling these modifications to users.
- **Pedigree and Modification Qualifiers**: Steve Springett and Philippe Ombredanne discussed adding a "modified" or "pedigree" qualifier to the specification to represent derivatives or variants, rather than overloading existing fields. This would address use cases where revalidation of certified devices is cost-prohibitive.
- **CISA and Tooling**: Michael Herzog and Steve Springett noted recent updates from CISA regarding SBOM minimum elements (https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/2026-minimum-elements-software-bill-materials-sbom). Philippe Ombredanne mentioned plans to release an open-source version of a JSON Schema builder tool to assist with development.
- **Community Office Hours**: Michael Herzog proposed establishing regular "office hours" to allow community members to discuss specific topics, reducing the need for attendance at formal TG2 meetings.
- **Documentation and Closing**: The meeting concluded with a discussion on documentation timelines, with Michael Herzog planning to draft documentation after the next TG2 meeting. They will use pull requests against the current specification HTML for PURL (https://github.com/Ecma-TC54/ECMA-427/blob/main/spec.html) and coordinate with Aki Rose Braun regarding the placement of ABNF grammar within the EC markup.
- **Clarification of Required and Recommended Behaviors**: Michael Herzog emphasized the importance of establishing a clear distinction between "required" and "recommended" behaviors within the project's documentation. They noted that the existing "how to parse" documentation for PURL contains a mix of these behaviors, and the team needs to align this content with requirements for canonical form. Michael Herzog suggested that this effort is largely focused on implementation and clarifying standards rather than making substantive changes to the procedures themselves.
- **Development of Normalization Documentation**: Michael Herzog proposed the creation of a "how to normalize" document, explaining that approximately 95% of the work being performed that does not follow the canonical form constitutes normalization.
- **Updates to the ECMA Standard**: Regarding the PURL ECMA standard, Michael Herzog clarified that the pending workload consists mostly of documentation and human-focused tasks, with only a handful of specific changes required for the ECMA standard itself.
- The meeting was adjourned.
